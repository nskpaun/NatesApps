Level 1 · The 60-second version

Chain Theory in a minute

The problem

Physics' best theory of particles, the Standard Model, works very well. But it has about two dozen numbers we have to measure because nothing explains them. The most famous is the fine-structure constant, α ≈ 1/137, which sets how strongly light and electric charge interact. Richard Feynman called it "a magic number that comes to us with no understanding by man."

The idea

AWG proposes that, at bottom, the universe is nothing but a finite number of tiny quantum objects linked in pairs by perfect entanglement, like the links of chainmail. Particles aren't vibrating strings (as in string theory); they're small patterns of chain. And the only way anything is ever "known" is by linking to it. There's no observer standing outside the network.

"Nothing exists but finitely many maximally entangled pairs of qudits, some qudits shared, known only by distilling them."

That sentence is the whole input. "Qudit" means a quantum bit that can have 2, 3, 4… states instead of just 2.

The headline result

Take the one special link where a 3-state qudit is entangled with a 2-state qudit. Count, in a precise way, every possible "measurement record" two reads can leave on it. The count produces this number:

(79 + 48√2 + 42√3 + 3√6) / 31104 = 1 / 137.035 999 189…Measured (CODATA 2022): 1 / 137.035 999 177(21). They agree to about 10 significant digits.

AWG conjectures that this number is the fine-structure constant. The arithmetic is machine-checked (in the Lean proof language). The claim that it's the real α is a conjecture, to be tested by future measurements.

Why it's interesting

  • No free knobs. Past attempts to "derive" 1/137 failed because they had something to tune. This one claims nothing is tuned.
  • It's falsifiable. The formula fixes every digit in advance. New experiments aim to be up to ten times more precise. If they drift away from 137.035 999 189…, the conjecture is dead.
  • It points further. The same link has exactly the symmetry group of the Standard Model, SU(3)×SU(2)×U(1). The 754-page compendium sketches how quantum mechanics, spacetime, gravity, three generations of matter, and more might also come out.

The honest caveats

  • It's brand new (Oct 23, 2026) and hasn't been vetted by other physicists yet.
  • "Numerology" has a long history: people have found many formulas close to 1/137. AWG argues his is different because it follows from stated rules, but whether those rules are the right rules is the open question.
  • Of the compendium's 407 graded results, 100 are called theorems, 109 are conditional on stated assumptions, and 198 are open. Many links to real particles are "by name only."

1. Who, what, when

Alexander D. Wissner-Gross is a physicist and computer scientist (PhD in physics from Harvard) and a regular voice on Peter Diamandis's Moonshots podcast. He writes the newsletter The Innermost Loop. In October 2026 he released three documents:

  • The alpha Letter: "A Parameter-Free Candidate for the Fine-Structure Constant from Entangled Qudits" (24 pages including the Supplemental Material), dated Oct 23, 2026. This is the sharp, short claim.
  • The compendium: "Chain Theory: Toward Fundamental Physics from Distilling Networks of Entangled Qudits. A Compendium of Preliminary Findings." Working draft, 754 pages, sixteen Parts plus appendices and 52 "instrument" chapters.
  • An introductory talk (slides) that walks through both.

All three are in the Library tab, in full.

2. The fifty-year drought

AWG frames the project historically. From 1900 to 1928, physics was rebuilt from the ground up: Planck's quantum (1900), special relativity (1905), Bohr's atom (1913), general relativity (1915), quantum mechanics (1925), and Dirac's equation (1928). Over the next decades those became the Standard Model, whose structure was essentially complete by the mid-1970s.

Since then, he argues, physics has had confirmation but no new structure. The W and Z bosons (1983), top quark (1995), Higgs boson (2012) and gravitational waves (2015) were all predicted decades earlier. The genuine surprises, neutrino masses and the accelerating expansion (both 1998), were explained by no theory and added parameters.

Faced with that, the field largely shifted from deriving the constants to selecting them: anthropic arguments, the string-theory landscape of many possible vacua, and the "swampland" program. AWG's stance is the opposite. Keep derivation as the goal, and look for new foundations instead.

3. Three clues hiding in plain sight

AWG argues that existing physics already contains three hints, each of which has been read as something else.

Clue 1 · A bound: finite information

Bekenstein (1981) showed a bounded region can hold only finitely much information. Holography ('t Hooft, Susskind), gravity from thermodynamics (Jacobson 1995), and "spacetime from entanglement" (Van Raamsdonk 2010) all build on this. Fields have infinitely many degrees of freedom and strings infinitely many modes. Lesson: build the world from finitely many quantum systems of finite dimension.

Clue 2 · A gauge group: the Standard Model's "ugly" symmetry

The Standard Model's symmetry group is SU(3)×SU(2)×U(1) (more precisely, divided by ℤ₆). Grand Unified Theories treated this lumpy product as a blemish to hide inside one bigger group (SU(5), SO(10)…). Those theories predicted proton decay and superpartners, and neither has been seen.

AWG's reading: the group is exactly the set of independent "basis changes" you can make at the two ends of one qutrit (3 states) perfectly entangled with one qubit (2 states). That gives SU(3) at one end, SU(2) at the other, and one leftover phase that belongs to the pair rather than either end. So the gauge group is a signature of a single entangled pair.

Clue 3 · A null result: particles show no parts

Every elementary particle looks like a point, even at the highest collider energies. Yet three generations repeating the same pattern of charges is exactly the sort of thing that usually means constituents (think periodic table → protons/neutrons → quarks).

Quantum information has a kind of correlation, bound entanglement (Horodecki, Horodecki & Horodecki, 1998), that is genuinely entangled yet can't be "distilled" into a usable entangled pair by any local operations. It first becomes possible when the two systems' dimensions multiply to more than 6. AWG's reading: the substructure of matter isn't undetectable, it's undistillable. Scattering would see a point, while a suitable "entanglement witness" could in principle show the structure.

4. The axiom, in plain words

Nothing exists but finitely many maximally entangled pairs of qudits, some qudits shared, known only by distilling them.

Unpacked:

  • Qudits. The building blocks are quantum systems with a finite number of states: qubits (2), qutrits (3), ququarts (4), and so on.
  • Maximally entangled pairs. They only come in perfectly entangled pairs, called links. Partial entanglement would introduce continuous numbers, which a fully discrete theory can't afford.
  • Some qudits shared. A qudit can be the end of several links, so pairs join into networks ("webs"). This is the chainmail.
  • Known only by distilling. The only thing that ever happens is "distillation": projecting two qudits into a perfectly entangled pair in one step. It's the same move as entanglement swapping in quantum networks. In a lab it succeeds only sometimes (probability 1/d²); in the theory it's the only thing that ever happens. Consequence: there is no external observer. To know something is to link to it.

The model also says time isn't assumed. Acts on separate qudits can be reordered freely, like rewriting 2·3·3·2 = 36 in different groupings. The compendium tries to count time as steps between acts (graded conditional).

Distillation as entanglement swapping: projecting qudits 2 and 3 into an entangled pair leaves 1 and 4 entangled, although they never touched.

5. Why "chain"?

The name has three sources (from the talk and preface):

  1. Chains, not strings. Particles are finite chainmail made of discrete links, not continuous strings.
  2. Free joints. Where pairs meet at a shared qudit, nothing fixes how the bases line up, just as two chain links can turn against each other without changing the chain. That freedom is the theory's gauge freedom.
  3. Read from one end. A distillation passes its basis outward along each link, so knowledge accumulates in a fixed order, "the way a chained ledger grows by linking each new entry to the last."

6. Six kinds of link, two you can see

A link's genre is the pair of its end dimensions. Conjecture 0 of the compendium restricts ends to dimension 2, 3 or 4, which gives six genres. Each is conjectured to correspond to a sector of physics:

GenreEndsd₁·d₂Readable?Conjectured sectorCompendium Part

(3,2)qutrit–qubit6readableelectromagnetism (the photon)VII

(4,4)ququart–ququart16darkthe HiggsXI

Why only two are readable: the axiom says two ends are "known" only if every consistent relation between them is one a distillation can carry. Classic math theorems (Størmer 1963, Woronowicz 1976, Choi 1975) show this holds exactly when d₁·d₂ ≤ 6. That's the same threshold where bound entanglement becomes possible. So only spacetime (2,2) and electromagnetism (3,2) can be observed directly. The other four are "dark" and are seen only at second order, through two distillations. At (3,3) the theory reads this as confinement: you never see a lone quark.

Note the elegant coincidence AWG leans on: the boundary between "readable" and "dark" (product ≤ 6) is a theorem of pure mathematics. It happens to split off exactly the two forces we observe directly at long range, light and gravity.

7. The number

Why pick α as the test? (From the talk.)

  • Measured, not derived. It's known to about one part in ten billion, yet the Standard Model simply takes it from experiment.
  • Hard to hit by accident. A simple value like Koide's 2/3 can come from many short formulas. Ten digits of 137.035 999… can't.
  • Nothing left to tune. Older closed forms (Wyler 1969/71, and others) foundered because some volume or normalization could be rescaled. A real derivation has to leave nothing to fix.

The result. Among all configurations of readable links, exactly one satisfies three conditions: it is a two-point function, it is read at the qutrit end, and every link carries an acting electric-type charge. That configuration is one shared qutrit with three links to qubits, two of them "read." Counting the measurement records two reads can leave gives 172 records, sorted by two symmetries into four groups of sizes 79, 48, 42 and 3, weighted 1, √2, √3 and √6. The total number of possible "directions" is 24·36·36 = 31104. So:

α = (79 + 48√2 + 42√3 + 3√6) / 311041/α = 137.035 999 189 566 155 029 183 744…

The comparison: CODATA 2022 recommends 1/α = 137.035 999 177 with uncertainty 21 in the last two digits. The candidate sits 0.6σ away, between the two most precise measurements. But those measurements disagree with each other: cesium recoil (2018) and rubidium recoil (2020) are in tension. So the comparison isn't decisive yet. Upcoming measurements (electron g−2 at Northwestern; atomic recoil in Berkeley and Paris) aim to be up to 10× more precise.

8. What else it claims

The compendium's abstract lists results at three grades:

Theorems the Standard Model gauge group SU(3)×SU(2)×U(1); conservation laws; the second law of thermodynamics; band structure and the Chern number; phase transitions; a distillation theorem for quantum computation; "three generations as a theorem of the valence" (box 83.1).

Conditional theorems the state space, Born rule, unitarity and uncertainty relation of quantum mechanics; the rotation and Lorentz groups; the signature of spacetime; parity, time reversal and CPT; the α candidate itself (as a physical identification); the electron's anomalous magnetic moment through third order; the hydrogen spectrum; parity violation; confinement; running of couplings; decay rates as finite sums with no renormalization; curvature; the cosmological term.

Partly open three generations, particle masses, and the "anatomies" (webs) of Standard Model particles.

Particles get proposed webs. For example, the electron is a core triangle of three qutrits (3,3), an in-leg (4,3) joining a "hub" to the core, hub legs (4,2), a qubit pair (2,2), and docks (3,2). The muon carries two hub assemblies and the tau three ("the lepton ladder"). The W and Z are one web read two different ways. A hadron is a "star": a junction qutrit carrying three quark bodies. All of these identifications are conjectures. Of 17 particles drawn on 16 webs, 1 rests on a theorem, 11 on conditional theorems, 2 on open results and 3 on results not in this release.

AWG's own caution about "theorem": many theorems are exhaustive checks over the finite set of webs the compendium records. For example, the second law is checked at all 228 one-act reads of fourteen bodies. That's "a census of a finite catalog, not a proof for every web."

Also: "names from the literature are correspondences by name." When a box says "the Higgs" or "the weak interaction," it is a statement about networks that he names after the physics. The physical identification is separate.

10. A skeptic's checklist

Questions worth holding while reading. These are mine, not claims from the sources.

  • Are the rules forced? The Letter shows (in its "What else the count could have given" table) that small rewordings of the definitions give 1/α of 135.89, 139.03, 146.52, 97.51, 180.84 and so on. AWG argues each alternative is excluded by a definition before comparing with α. A skeptic will ask whether the definitions were chosen knowing the target. Two of the alternatives land within 1%.
  • Lean checks arithmetic, not physics. The talk says so directly: the Lean file checks that the definitions produce the number, not that they're the right physics. The photon conjecture "appears nowhere in it."
  • The link carries hypercharge, not electric charge. The talk notes the object's charge is the hypercharge direction, and "its passage to electric charge, together with spacetime and helicity, lies beyond the Letter." That's a real gap between the math object and the photon.
  • Running of α. α changes with energy. The Letter targets α(0), the zero-momentum (Thomson-limit) value, via the on-shell scheme. That choice is principled, but it's another place to check the reasoning.
  • Look-elsewhere effect. Ten digits is a lot of evidence if the formula wasn't selected from many candidates. How you weigh it depends on how much freedom you think the construction had.
  • Peer review and conflicts. At release this was a self-published preprint and draft. Secondary coverage notes AWG co-founded Physical Superintelligence (psi.inc), and that the work was developed with AI assistance. Independent audits were being called for online.
  • The decisive test is coming. If next-generation α(0) measurements converge on 137.035 999 189 6…, that's very hard to dismiss. If they converge elsewhere, the conjecture fails cleanly. That's a strength.

Level 3 · The math of the α number

How the count works

Assumes some linear algebra and the basics of quantum states (kets, tensor products). Follows the alpha Letter and the talk; page references point into the Letter and the talk.

1. The definitions

A link of type (d₁, d₂) joins two qudits in the maximally entangled state on their common support, of dimension m = min(d₁, d₂):

|ωm⟩ = (1/√m) Σi=1..m |i⟩|i⟩

Because (A ⊗ 1)|ω⟩ = (1 ⊗ Aᵀ)|ω⟩, the pair has the same form in every basis (with the conjugate basis at the far end). A matching, a change of basis by a unitary U, acts as U ⊗ Ū and leaves the pair unchanged. Each pair has its own matching, so nothing aligns the bases of a shared qudit's copies. That freedom at the joins is the gauge freedom.

WordDefinition (paraphrasing the talk/Letter)
quditA finite-dimensional quantum system of dimension ≥ 2. A 1-dimensional system can't be entangled, so it ends no pair.
sharedA qudit that ends two or more pairs, carried as one copy per pair (as at a PEPS site, so monogamy isn't violated). The copies are still one qudit, so exchanging two pairs moves only their far ends.
distillProject two qudits of dimensions d₁, d₂ onto Pm = |ωm⟩⟨ωm| on their common support. One copy, one joint projection, no failure branch.
readAn act at one end of a pair that distills the two ends in a basis of that end (one read per each of its d+1 mutually unbiased bases) and passes the basis along the pair. The basis reaches the far end whole, so a read is taken at the larger end.
knownWhat its reads fix jointly, under every matching and every exchange of reads or pairs. Two anticommuting reads also fix their product, whose sign flips with their order.

"To fix" means determined with certainty from a single copy, not estimated over many. Certainty leaves nothing open, and that gives the read cap.

Linking two pairs: distilling 2 with 3 makes 1 and 4 a pair, as in entanglement swapping. Ordinary QM gives this branch probability 1/d². The axiom admits no other branch: "the complementary outcome is no configuration of the axiom, so the projection is the act itself." In a lab, that's postselection. (Postselection as a law has been proposed before, for black hole final states and closed timelike curves.)

Strand picture: a pair is d strands, one per term of |ω⟩, each with amplitude 1/√d. A path from 1 to 4 crosses three strands, picking up 1/(d√d), and its d paths sum to (1/d)|ω⟩₁₄. Remember the factor √d = d·(1/√d). It returns as the weights.

2. Three consequences settle the measurement theory

  • The twirl. The pair is invariant (envariant, Zurek 2003) under every matching. So every readout is invariant under their average, the isotropic twirl.
  • One copy. Pairs are finite and distilling creates no qudit, so a read acts once on the one copy of its link. What matters is what a single read fixes, not asymptotic distillability.
  • A trace. Operators invariant under every matching are spanned by 1 and Pm (Horodecki & Horodecki 1999), so a read delivers its pair projector and nothing finer. The readout of a configuration is ⟨M⟩ = TrR M / dim R, where R holds the whole operator space of each read link and the traceless part of each unread link.

3. The read cap: readable iff d₁d₂ ≤ 6

A read fixes two ends only when every consistent relation between them, meaning every positive map between their operator algebras, is of a kind distillations carry. Reads compose only decomposable maps (sums of completely positive and co-completely-positive maps). So a genre is readable iff every positive map between its ends is decomposable:

TypePositive mapsSource
(2,2)all decomposableStørmer 1963
(3,2)all decomposableWoronowicz 1976
(4,2)some not decomposableWoronowicz 1976
(3,3)some not decomposableChoi 1975

Non-decomposable maps embed in every d₁d₂ ≥ 8, and no product of integers ≥ 2 equals 7. So readable = (2,2) and (3,2), and only (3,2) has unequal ends. This is the same boundary as the Peres–Horodecki criterion: the partial transpose detects all entanglement exactly when d₁d₂ ≤ 6. The talk emphasizes that "no state is bound entangled" in the model. What no distillation carries is a relation, not a state.

4. What must be computed: the on-shell coupling

For a massless spin-1 field coupled to a conserved abelian charge, the zero-transfer coupling is universal (Weinberg 1964–65). The Ward identity Z₁ = Z₂ reduces it to vacuum polarization: α(0) = Z₃ α_bare. Heavy charged states decouple from this limit (Appelquist–Carazzone 1975). So the model must supply:

  1. one object with a conserved abelian charge obeying a Ward identity;
  2. a two-point function with its pole at zero transfer;
  3. its dressed-over-bare two-point function at that pole (the ratio of residues, Z₃).

In the model: α_bare = 1 (the bare unit), Z₂/Z₁ = 1 by conservation of the count, and Z₃ = TrRM / N. Identifying the normalized readout with the on-shell two-point function is "one definitional identification," used in no proof.

5. The central charge picks one readable genre

The identification algebra of a link, the infinitesimal basis changes at its two ends, is su(d₁) ⊕ su(d₂) ⊕ u(1). Its center is spanned by

Q = i·diag(d₂·𝟙, −d₁·𝟙)

which acts on the whole link, pair ket included, as the scalar i(d₂ − d₁). That's zero at (2,2) and i at (3,2). So exactly one readable genre carries an acting central charge.

At (3,2) the algebra is su(3) ⊕ su(2) ⊕ u(1), the Lie algebra of S(U(3)×U(2)) ≅ (SU(3)×SU(2)×U(1))/ℤ₆, the Standard Model gauge group. Q = 6iY, with Y = diag(½, ½; −⅓, −⅓, −⅓), the hypercharge generator. The link carries (3, 2) with Y = 1/6, which are the quantum numbers of the left-handed quark doublet.

(That S(U(3)×U(2)) is the stabilizer of a 3+2 split in SU(5) is known; see Baez & Huerta 2010. The new part is reading it as the symmetry of one entangled pair.)

6. Three conditions leave one configuration

Theorem 1 (uniqueness). Among configurations of readable links at a shared qudit, exactly one (a) is a two-point function, (b) is read at the qutrit end, and (c) carries an acting central charge on every link: a shared qutrit with three links to qubits, two of them read.
  • Acting charge: (2,2) links carry none, so only (2,3) links remain.
  • End rule: a read reaches its far end whole, so (2,3) links are read at the qutrit, and the shared qudit is a qutrit.
  • Two-point function: two read links (the two insertions) and one unread link of the same type (the state). Reading the third would be a third insertion.

7. The denominator: N = 24 · 36 · 36 = 31104

  • Unread link: 24 = (3²−1)(2²−1). A traceless "letter" at each end, no blanks. This is the connected part of its pair projector, the state.
  • Each read link: 36 = (3·2)². The whole operator space of the link, blank (identity) included: 9 × 4.

The bare insertion (the identity) weighs each of the 31104 directions once. The dressed insertion weighs only the 172 records, each by the strands it runs over.

8. The 172 measurement records

Each read places a letter at either end of its link: a Gell-Mann matrix at the qutrit (8 choices) or a Pauli matrix at the qubit (3 choices). There are four slots: Q₁, q₁ (read 1) and Q₂, q₂ (read 2), with Q₁ and Q₂ being two copies of the one shared qutrit. Slot sizes: (8, 3, 8, 3).

A record is two letters at two different slots. Two at the same slot would multiply into a single insertion. Picture the slots as corners of a square and records as edges:

The mirror (swap read 1 ↔ read 2, i.e. Q₁↔Q₂, q₁↔q₂) fixes 11 products: the same letter on both copies (8) or on both qubits (3). It pairs the other 158 into 79 symmetric + 79 antisymmetric combinations.

9. Two commuting exchanges set the weights

  • X, the exchange of reads, acts on records with two letters of one type (73 products + the 3 commutators). A read is an act at the end where it is taken, so X acts at the qutrit wherever the letters sit. It carries d = 3.
  • Y, the exchange of links, leaves the shared qutrit in place (its copies are one qudit) and swaps the two read qubits. It acts on the 96 products with one slot at each end, plus the 3 commutators. It carries d = 2.

A record that survives an exchange (returns times its own sign) runs over the d matched directions at amplitude 1/√d: d·(1/√d) = √d. Formally, kr = ½(2 − Xr)(3 − Yr) and the weight is √kr. It's √k and not k because a record carries two letters (two insertions), while k is a four-letter, four-point quantity.

10. The fast check: symmetric and antisymmetric squares

Reference

Glossary

Plain-language definitions. For AWG's own ~200-term "Rosetta table," which pairs each Chain Theory noun with its nearest standard term, see compendium pp. 22–33. The alpha Letter also has a glossary.

α, α(0), fine-structure constant
The dimensionless strength of electromagnetism, ≈ 1/137.036. α(0) is its value at zero momentum transfer (the Thomson limit), the one measured by low-energy experiments. α grows slowly at higher energies ("running").
Axiom
The single input: "Nothing exists but finitely many maximally entangled pairs of qudits, some qudits shared, known only by distilling them."
Bound entanglement
Entanglement that's real (certifiable by a witness) but cannot be distilled into pure entangled pairs by local operations and classical communication, from any number of copies. Possible only when d₁d₂ > 6. AWG's model for why particle substructure is invisible.
Bra end / ket end
In the "copy model," every link joins one ket end and one bra end, like indices of a tensor.
CODATA
The international committee that recommends best values for fundamental constants every four years. CODATA 2022: 1/α = 137.035 999 177(21).
Commutator record
One of the 3 extra records at the qubits: two reads in different bases anticommute (XZ = −ZX), so their commutator is fixed too, with a sign set by the order of reads. Weight √6.
Conditional theorem
A result derived under conditions that are named in its box. A composite claim takes the lowest grade in its chain.
Confinement
The fact that quarks are never seen alone. In Chain Theory, read as the (3,3) genre being "dark": no read survives at first order.
Dark genre
A link type whose ends are related by some non-decomposable positive map (d₁d₂ ≥ 8), so a single read can't fix them. Seen only at second order. (4,2), (3,3), (4,3), (4,4).
Decomposable map
A positive map that's a sum of a completely positive map and a completely positive map composed with transpose. Every positive map is decomposable in dimensions 2×2 and 2×3 (Størmer, Woronowicz), but not beyond.
Distill / distillation
In the axiom: project two qudits onto the maximally entangled state on their common support, in one step, with no failure branch. Equivalent to the projection in entanglement swapping. In standard QI, "distillation" means extracting good pairs from many noisy copies; AWG generalizes it.
Dressed vs. bare insertion
The bare insertion is the identity, weighing every one of N directions once. The dressed insertion M weighs only what the reads fix (the 172 records, weighted). Their ratio is the readout.
Entanglement swapping
Measuring qudits 2 and 3 of pairs (1,2) and (3,4) in an entangled basis leaves 1 and 4 entangled. The basic move of quantum repeaters, and the act of the axiom.
Exchange of links (Y)
The symmetry that swaps two links at the shared qutrit (moving their far-end qubits). Carries d = 2, giving weight √2 to symmetric records it acts on.
Exchange of reads (X)
Swaps the order/identity of the two reads. Acts at the qutrit (where reads are taken), carries d = 3, giving weight √3.
Fine-structure star
The configuration of Theorem 1: a qutrit of degree three with three (3,2) arms, two read and one held (unread).
Fix
To determine with certainty from a single copy, not to estimate statistically over many.
Gauge group
The symmetry group of the Standard Model, (SU(3)×SU(2)×U(1))/ℤ₆. In Chain Theory, the group of basis changes at the two ends of a qutrit–qubit pair, S(U(3)×U(2)).
Gell-Mann matrices
The 8 traceless Hermitian 3×3 matrices that form a basis for su(3). The "letters" at a qutrit.
Genre
A link's type, the pair of its end dimensions written larger first: (2,2), (3,2), (4,2), (3,3), (4,3), (4,4).
Hub, hub assembly
A recurring sub-web in the conjectured particle webs. Each fermion generation adds one hub assembly (electron 1, muon 2, tau 3: "the lepton ladder").
Hypercharge (Y)
The U(1) charge of the Standard Model before electroweak symmetry breaking. The central charge of the (3,2) link is 6iY.
Known
What reads fix jointly, unchanged under every matching and every exchange of reads or pairs.
Lean 4
A proof assistant / programming language that machine-checks mathematical proofs. Used here to verify the finite computations (64 theorems tied to Lean declarations).
Letter
(1) A traceless operator basis element at a slot: Gell-Mann at a qutrit, Pauli at a qubit. (2) "The alpha Letter," the short paper with the α result.
Link / pair
Two qudits in the maximally entangled state |ω_m⟩ on their common support.
Matching
A change of basis U on a pair's support, acting as U⊗Ū. It leaves the pair unchanged, which is the gauge freedom at the joins.
Maximally entangled
|ω_m⟩ = (1/√m) Σ|i⟩|i⟩. Each end on its own is completely random, while together they're perfectly correlated.
Monogamy of entanglement
A system maximally entangled with one partner can't be entangled with another. Why a shared qudit is carried as one copy per pair.
Mutually unbiased bases
Bases in which measuring one tells you nothing about the other. A d-level system has up to d+1 of them; one read per MUB.
On-shell scheme
The convention defining α by the physical (zero-momentum) photon coupling: α(0) = Z₃·α_bare after the Ward identity.
Open result
A statement carried to a named step that remains open: neither established nor excluded.
Pauli matrices
X, Y, Z, the 3 traceless Hermitian 2×2 matrices. The "letters" at a qubit.
PEPS
Projected entangled pair states: a tensor-network construction where each site holds several virtual qudits, each maximally entangled with a neighbor. The model of a shared qudit.
Photon Conjecture
That the qutrit–qubit link of Theorem 1 is the elementary link of the photon, so its readout is α(0).
Positive map
A linear map on operators that sends positive operators to positive operators. Every "consistent relation" between two ends is one.
Qubit / qutrit / ququart / qudit
Quantum systems with 2 / 3 / 4 / d states. AWG also calls them registers two, three, four.
Read
An act at one end of a pair, distilling the two ends in a basis of that end, passing the basis along. Taken at the larger end.
Read cap
The theorem that a genre is readable iff d₁d₂ ≤ 6: only (2,2) and (3,2).
Readout
⟨M⟩ = Tr_R M / dim R, the dressed-over-bare normalized trace.
Record
Two letters at two different slots, the two insertions of a two-point function. 169 products + 3 commutators = 172.
Rosetta table
The compendium's instrument mapping each Chain Theory noun to its nearest standard concept.
Saturation, seed, sink
Compendium machinery. A seeded web is grown by moves ("waves") until it reaches a sink, a stable endpoint. Particles are proposed to be sinks; decay is a forward edge out of a non-sink.
Slot
One of the four ends where letters sit: Q₁, q₁, Q₂, q₂.
Standard Model
The established theory of quarks, leptons, and the electromagnetic, weak and strong forces plus the Higgs. About two dozen measured parameters.
Twirl
Averaging over all matchings U⊗Ū. Since pairs are invariant, every readout is twirl-invariant, so reads see only 𝟙 and the pair projector.
Ward identity
A consequence of charge conservation (Z₁ = Z₂) that makes the charge's renormalization depend only on the photon's vacuum polarization Z₃.
Web
A network of pairs joined at shared qudits. The nearest standard term is a graph state / tensor network. Particles are conjectured to be specific webs.
σ (sigma)
Standard deviations of measurement uncertainty. 0.6σ means the candidate is well within the error bar.
