What is in the render

  • Checked against the study. With a white band, the render puts 81 nits on the ribbon from the seat; the optics study's own tracer predicts 81. Two independent renderers, same geometry.
  • The stand. The cove continues over a rounded lip into a wall raked back 23° and lands on the deck just in front of the screen plane. It runs the full width with no gap: the stowed keyboard and mouse clear it by 11 mm or more. The old neck stays behind it as the load path. No band light reaches the wall, so the ribbon is the same as before.
  • The sill is dark satin, like the panel's own chin. In titanium its bevel caught the room light and outshone the ribbon directly beneath it.
  • The amber LEDs are off (neck bar, under-glow, charge marks), so every warm glow in these images comes from the screen.
  • Exposure. The dark and home-office frames are exposed 1.4× over the panel's white, the office frame 1×, the window frame 0.5×, the way a camera would follow the room. Within a frame nothing is adjusted: toggle the ribbon to see exactly what it adds.
  • Still assumed: the finer-brush roughness (no measured data), a 16 mm chin, unpolarised light, and a simple room (one window, a ceiling wash, a low sun patch on the wall).
