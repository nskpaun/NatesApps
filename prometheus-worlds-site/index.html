Prometheus Worlds

Photograph a room, or describe one. The app builds a 3D world you can look around, relight, and drop balls into. Every model runs on your Mac. The server listens only there.

This is the public name. The Mac app is still titled Nate’s Worlds.

New world

Two ways in, one world at a time

Choose New world…, then From your photos or From a prompt. A panel in the corner shows progress. You can cancel. A cancelled prompt build resumes from the views already painted.

01

From your photos

Pick 20 to 60 overlapping photos of one room: JPG, PNG, or HEIC. Walk around while you shoot, instead of standing in one spot. It takes a few minutes.

  1. COLMAP works out where each photo was taken.
  2. msplat trains a Gaussian splat on the Mac’s GPU.
  3. Depth Pro estimates a depth for each photo. Those maps are scaled to the solved points and fused into a coloured mesh.

02

From a prompt

Write a layout sentence and a style sentence. The room can be small or a large hall. Name the big things: furniture, stages, benches, columns. The plan starts from simple solids.

  1. Qwen3.5 9B writes a plan: room size, and pieces against walls, at points, in corners, or in rings and arcs. A piece is a box, an upright cylinder, or a named piece of furniture. Code keeps the plan inside the room and on the floor.
  2. If the Hunyuan3D shape model is installed, up to six kinds of object become a mesh instead of staying a box. Qwen chooses which kinds deserve one. One picture of each kind is painted on a plain backdrop, and Hunyuan3D turns it into a mesh fitted to its block. Each kind adds about a minute. Without the model, or if a shape fails, that object stays a box.
  3. The blockout, with those shapes in place of their boxes, is rendered as depth. Z-Image Turbo with a depth ControlNet paints the views so they follow it. Each view is given the pixels earlier views already show, so it stays the same room.
  4. msplat trains a splat. The blockout, including any generated shapes, is the mesh and the collider.

Detail in the dialog: Quick, about 14 minutes. Standard, about 20 minutes. Full, larger pictures, about 40 minutes. Quick and Standard paint at 768×576. Full paints at 1024×768. Copies of a generated kind are identical, and the side the picture did not show is guessed. The Hunyuan3D licence does not cover use in the EU, the UK, or South Korea.

The fire stays home

Nothing about the world has to leave the Mac that made it.

The app is a window. It starts a private server in the project folder and loads the viewer from 127.0.0.1. The server binds to that address only.

After the model weights are on disk, the pipelines are built not to call a cloud service. That has not yet been tested with Wi-Fi turned off.

An Apple Silicon Mac, macOS 14 or later. Developed on an M4 Pro with 64 GB. The pipelines stay under about 32 GB by running one model at a time, and one world is built at a time. Painting peaks around 18 GB. The weights take tens of gigabytes on first use.

Qwen3.5 9B
Turns the layout sentence into a furniture plan, through Ollama.
Z-Image Turbo
Paints the views with Z-Image-Turbo-Fun-Controlnet-Union 2.1, the distilled 8-step file, through mflux.
Depth Pro
Depth for each photo, also through mflux.
Hunyuan3D, optional
Turns one picture of an object into a mesh, through Hunyuan3D-Swift. About 20 seconds and 5.5 GB for a kind. Skip it and the object stays a box.
COLMAP, msplat, Open3D
Cameras, the Gaussian splat, and the fused mesh. No model weights of their own.
three.js, Spark, Rapier
The viewer: splats on screen, and balls that fall and bounce.

In the viewer

Look, relight, then join the rooms

The toolbar is small on purpose. Splat, mesh, or collider. Drop balls. Night mode and a lamp. A new world, or several worlds stitched into one.

01

Drop balls

The mesh is a collider. Balls fall, bounce, and come to rest on the desk, the floor, the furniture. In a stitched world they can roll through a doorway into the next room.

Photo meshes have holes where no photo looked. A flat safety floor catches a ball that falls through.

02

Night mode and a lamp

Tick Night mode + lamp. The splat is multiplied by the light that reaches the mesh, so the detail of the room survives the dark. Drag the lamp. It casts shadows on the mesh.

There is no export of a relit splat, and no flythrough video from the app. You are in the room, looking.

03

Stitch worlds

Stitch worlds… shows your worlds from above, ceilings hidden. Tick the ones to include. Click a world, then drag the arrows to move it, or switch to Rotate. Snap moves in 25 cm steps and turns in 15° steps. Scroll to zoom.

Set a room against another and the walls meet. A green block marks the doorway: 1 metre wide, 2.1 metres tall. Name it and choose Save as one world. The stitched world keeps each room and where it sits, and loads the originals side by side.

Doorways are cut only between invented rooms whose facing walls touch, through the splat, the mesh, and the collider. A world made from photos can sit in the layout. It does not get a doorway, because its walls are not known exactly. A doorway also cuts whatever is standing there, including furniture.

You orbit. You do not walk yet.

Drag to look, scroll to move. That is an orbit camera. Moving through a dungeon on foot is not built. Invented rooms are boxes from 3 to 24 metres across: no curved walls, sloped roofs, stairs, or tiers. An object without a generated shape is painted onto a simple form, so it reads from the front and looks flatter from the side. Invented ceilings are a plain light colour.

On your Mac

Build the app from the source.

There is no store listing. From the local-worlds folder, install the tools, set up the Python environments and the viewer, then run app/build.sh. That produces Nate’s Worlds.app.

The app is signed for the Mac it was built on. It remembers the project folder, so you rebuild it if that folder moves. You can also skip the app and run thread/server.py, then open the viewer at 127.0.0.1.
